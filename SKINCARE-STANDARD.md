# ROOTED Skincare Standard

Specified by J, August 2026. This governs every product in Personal Care.

---

## Core philosophy

Healthy skin does not require a complicated routine. The best skincare products **work with
the skin** rather than constantly trying to correct it.

> **The goal is not to use the most products. The goal is to use the fewest products that
> consistently support healthy skin.**

ROOTED prioritises: healthy skin barrier · minimal ingredients · ingredient transparency ·
proven safety · high-quality raw materials · practical effectiveness · low toxic burden ·
long-term skin health over short-term cosmetic effects.

**No seed oil should be present.** The base requirement is minimal ingredients that are whole
food or naturally derived.

---

## The hierarchy — fix the foundation before adding actives

**Tier 1 — Barrier support.** Every product should first protect and strengthen the skin
barrier: moisturisers, gentle cleansers, lip balms, tallow balms, simple facial oils.
*Without a healthy barrier, most "active" products become less effective and more
irritating.*

**Tier 2 — Protection.** Avoiding repeated sunburn · non-nano zinc sunscreen when
appropriate · gentle cleansing · adequate hydration · moisturising after bathing.

**Tier 3 — Targeted treatments.** Only after the basics are in place: acne treatments,
retinoids, vitamin C, azelaic acid, niacinamide, exfoliants.

---

## Green flags

**Ingredients:** short lists · organic where practical · grass-fed tallow · shea butter ·
cocoa butter · mango butter · jojoba oil · squalane · beeswax · glycerin · aloe vera · oat ·
calendula · chamomile · non-nano zinc oxide · vitamin E · natural clay · colloidal oatmeal

**Formulation:** fragrance-free preferred · essential oils only when clearly disclosed and
appropriately dosed · pH appropriate · minimal preservatives · stable formulations ·
transparent sourcing

**Packaging, in order of preference:** 1. Glass 2. Aluminium 3. Stainless steel 4. HDPE.
Avoid unnecessary plastic where practical.

---

## Red flags — automatic downgrades

**Fragrance:** "fragrance" · "parfum" · proprietary fragrance blends

**High-risk ingredients:** PFAS · formaldehyde releasers · methylisothiazolinone (MI) ·
methylchloroisothiazolinone (MCI) · benzisothiazolinone (BIT) · excessive synthetic dyes

**Unnecessary additives:** glitter · artificial colours · strong perfumes · cooling agents
without purpose · heavy fragrance masking

**Misleading marketing** — reduces confidence even when the formula is fine:
"chemical-free" · "toxin-free" · "miracle cream" · "anti-aging breakthrough" · "detox cream"

## Scoring weights

| Weight | Category | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| **30%** | Ingredient Quality | Are the ingredients well chosen, skin-supportive, and *necessary*? |
| **20%** | Ingredient Transparency | Full disclosure, clear concentrations where appropriate, no proprietary blends |
| **20%** | Safety Profile | Low irritation, minimal endocrine concern, low allergen burden, no unnecessary sensitisers |
| **20%** | Performance | Does it actually work without relying on harsh ingredients? |
| **10%** | Packaging | Glass, aluminium, HDPE preferred; avoid excessive plastic |

**J's stated priority order when these conflict:**
1. Ingredient simplicity
2. Third-party certification
3. Fragrance-free / low-irritant
4. Packaging

## Open question — the "no seed oil" rule applied to topicals

The dietary case against seed oils is about oxidation and omega-6 load **when eaten**.
Topically the picture differs: sunflower and safflower oil are high in linoleic acid, a
normal component of healthy skin barrier lipids, and some dermatology research favours
sunflower oil over olive oil for barrier repair.

J's ruling is that **no seed oil should be present**, within the broader minimal-ingredient
frame. That is the standard applied here.

**One existing listing conflicts** — see below.

---

## ⚠️ Audit finding on existing products

**Dr. Bronner's Peppermint Pure-Castile Bar Soap — currently Tier 1.** Its ingredient list
includes **Organic Hemp Seed Oil**. Hemp seed oil is a seed-derived, high-PUFA oil. It is not
on the Seed Oil Free Alliance's disallowed list (canola, soybean, sunflower, corn,
cottonseed, safflower, grapeseed, rice bran, peanut, flaxseed) — but flaxseed is, and hemp is
compositionally its close relative.

**Needs J's ruling: does the no-seed-oil rule cover hemp seed oil?** The answer affects this
listing's tier and will recur.

**Two other existing listings, checked and consistent:**
- *Primally Pure Everything Balm* (T1) — tallow, olive oil, emu oil, beeswax, disclosed
  essential oils. Consistent with the standard.
- *Vanicream Moisturizing Cream* (T3) — a synthetic formulation (petrolatum, propylene
  glycol, PEG-30 stearate, BHT). Far from "minimal, naturally derived", but Tier 3 is
  *Budget / Transitional* and it is genuinely fragrance-free and dermatologist-recommended
  for reactive skin. The tier is doing its job; no change proposed.
