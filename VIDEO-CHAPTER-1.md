# ROOTED — Chapter 1 Video Production Plan

**Format:** 50–60 seconds, vertical 9:16, for Instagram Reels / TikTok / YouTube Shorts
**Cast:** J (D1 baseball, USC) and Ben Centeno (D1 lacrosse, Villanova)
**Series title:** *(placeholder — see "Naming the series" at the end)*

---

## First, the strategic inversion

Their video works on **"we know nothing."** Yours cannot use that, and shouldn't try — you have a
built product, 469 rated items and 715 verified listings. Claiming ignorance would be false and
an audience would smell it.

**The equivalent contrarian hook for you is the opposite claim:**

> *We were supposed to be the healthiest people in the room. We were wrong.*

That is contrarian, specific, and only you two can say it. Two Division One athletes admitting
the "healthy" framework they were handed was wrong is a genuine reversal — and it earns the
right to sell a solution in a way "we have no experience" never does.

**Their formula:** vulnerability → nostalgia → contrast → open loop
**Your formula:** **credibility → disillusionment → contrast → proof → open loop**

You keep their pacing and text treatment. You replace "we're clueless" with "we were lied to,
and we did something about it."

## The script, beat by beat

Timings are targets. Read it out loud with a stopwatch before you film.

### BEAT 1 — Hook · 0:00–0:07

**Shot:** Both of you, direct to camera, outdoors, natural light. Standing, not sitting.
Chest-up framing, both in frame. Handheld, slight movement.

| Speaker | Line |
|---|---|
| **J** | "I played Division One baseball at USC." |
| **BEN** | "I played Division One lacrosse at Villanova." |
| **J** | "We were supposed to be the healthiest guys in the room." |
| **BOTH** *(overlapping, fast)* | "We were wrong." |

**On-screen text:** `WE WERE SUPPOSED TO BE THE HEALTHY ONES`

> **Why this order:** the credentials land first because they buy you the right to the claim.
> Front-loading "D1" in the first two seconds also stops the scroll — it is a specific,
> verifiable fact, not an adjective.

**Critical:** the first cut must land by **1.5 seconds.** Cut between "USC" and "Villanova."

### BEAT 4 — The turn · 0:28–0:38

**Shot:** Back to direct-to-camera. Slower. Let this one breathe — the pace change *is* the
emphasis.

| Speaker | Line |
|---|---|
| **J** | "So we started reading labels. Then calling farms." |
| **BEN** | "Then testing everything ourselves." |
| **J** | "Two years later we've rated four hundred and sixty-nine products." |
| **BEN** | "And verified seven hundred places to actually buy real food." |
| **J** | "Nobody paid us to list a single one." |

**On-screen text:** `469 PRODUCTS RATED` → `715 PLACES VERIFIED` → `ZERO PAID PLACEMENTS`

> **This beat is your whole differentiator.** Their video has no product clarity by design.
> Yours must have it by 30 seconds, because your credibility *is* the product. The
> no-pay-to-play line is the single strongest sentence in the script — it is the thing no
> competitor can copy.

| # | Shot | Notes |

| 2 | Both, one step tighter | Series branding |

| 4 | Ben alone, medium | Cutaway coverage |

| 7 | Both, tight, J only | Final line |

**Location:** somewhere with depth and real light. A rooftop works because theirs did, but a
farm, a market, or a kitchen would be *more yours*. **A farm would beat their rooftop** — it
puts the proof behind you while you talk.

### B-roll to shoot

- Reading a label closely, hands only — **shoot this five different ways, you'll use it a lot**
- Phone screen showing the ROOTED map
- Walking a farm, produce, livestock
- A farmers market stall
- Cooking in cast iron
- Both of you talking to a farmer *(gold — get one of these)*
- Laptop, catalogue on screen, scrolling

### Archive to dig up

- **Game footage** — both sports, ideally action
- Team photos, uniforms, locker rooms
- **Training-table or team-meal photos** *(the money shot for Beat 3 — check camera rolls)*
- Graduation
- Internship-era: office desk, laptop, commute

## Editing — how to actually cut it

**Use CapCut.** It's free, mobile or desktop, and built for exactly this. Premiere or Final Cut
if you already know them. **Claude Design cannot edit video** — it's a design canvas, not an
NLE, so it won't help here. *(See "Where Claude Design does help" below.)*

### Pacing rules

| Section | Cut length |
|---|---|
| Hook (0–7s) | 1–2s |
| Montage (13–28s) | **0.5–1.2s** |
| The turn (28–38s) | 2–3s — deliberately slower |
| Contrast (38–48s) | 1–1.5s |
| Close | One shot, no cuts |

**The pace change at Beat 4 is the most important edit in the video.** Everything speeds up,
then drops. That drop is what makes the numbers land.

### Text overlays

- **All caps. Heavy sans-serif.** Their bold sans is the single most replicable element
- **Same position every time** — lower third or centred, pick one and never move it
- **Appear on the cut**, not between cuts
- **Hold 1–1.5s**, then hard out — no fades
- High contrast: white text, or ROOTED terracotta `#B5563F` with a subtle dark scrim

### Audio

- **Use original audio.** Your voices are the asset — a trending sound would flatten it
- Music underneath at **15–20%**, building through the montage, **dropping out entirely at Beat
  4** so "four hundred and sixty-nine" lands in near-silence
- **Record voiceover separately** on a phone in a closet with clothes around you. Better than
  any on-camera mic you own

### Colour

Warm grade to match the ROOTED terracotta palette. Slightly lifted blacks, warm highlights.
**Do not over-grade** — the DIY authenticity is the point.

## Where Claude Design *does* help

Not for editing, but genuinely useful for:

- **Designing the text overlay style** — mock the exact type treatment, sizing and placement
  once, then rebuild it in CapCut and reuse it for every chapter
- **The end card** — logo lockup, URL, chapter title
- **A thumbnail/cover frame**
- **A series title card template** you reuse across every chapter

That's real work and worth doing before you film, because knowing your text style in advance
changes how you frame shots — you'll leave headroom for it.

## Naming the series

Theirs is *"As of Right Now."* Yours needs one, and it should carry the same
documentary-in-progress feeling. Candidates:

- **"Rooted in Progress"** — plain, works
- **"The Standard"** — ties directly to the philosophy, feels heavier
- **"Reading the Labels"** — literal, and a good running gag as chapters accumulate
- **"Before You Buy"** — implies utility, positions every chapter as useful

**My pick: "The Standard."** It's the only one that would still work in three years, and it
makes the pay-to-play refusal the identity of the whole series rather than a line in one video.

## What to fix that their video got wrong

Their own weakness, from the breakdown: **no product clarity, all energy and no substance.**

Your Beat 4 fixes that at the 30-second mark. **Do not cut it for pace.** If the video runs
long, trim the montage instead — the numbers are the thing nobody else can say.

**Second fix:** their montage is all extreme sports, which reads as a travel vlog. **Yours must
include the work** — a farm, a label, a phone screen. Roughly 60/40 athletic archive to
actual-work footage.
